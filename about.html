About This Project

This site is an attempt to bring dragon boat regatta results from many organizers, cities, and seasons into one consistent, searchable database. The goal is not to declare absolute rankings, but to make it easier to explore results, spot patterns, and understand how teams perform across different events and conditions.

Why Comparisons Are Tricky

On the surface, two 500 m races look identical. In reality, race conditions vary significantly from regatta to regatta and even from heat to heat on the same day. Because of this, a direct 1:1 comparison between two race times is not always fair or meaningful.

Here are some of the main factors that influence race times:

  • Saltwater vs freshwater: Seawater density is influenced by salinity (and temperature), and higher salinity generally increases density. That difference can slightly change buoyancy and drag, and can nudge times. (NOAA JetStream: Sea Water)
  • Weather and wind: Wind shapes waves and can influence surface currents, which changes how “fast” or “slow” a course feels depending on direction and exposure. (NOAA Ocean Service Education: Winds, Waves, Currents)
  • Course layout: Some courses are fully straight; others have bends, sheltered and exposed sections, or varying current patterns.
  • Water conditions: Chop, wake, and current can change how efficiently a crew can apply power.
  • Timing systems: Regattas vary in timing technology and procedures. Some use photo-finish and fully automated systems; others may be manual or hybrid, and precision and rounding can differ. (World Rowing: Rules of Racing (PDF))
  • Crew composition: Teams often change lineups between races, days, and regattas. A “team” name does not always mean the exact same paddlers.
  • Race format: Heats, semifinals, and finals are not always raced the same way. Crews may manage effort differently across rounds.

Because of all of this, this site is best used to explore patterns over many races, rather than to make absolute claims from a single time on a single day.

How You Should Think About the Data

More meaningful comparisons usually follow this process:

  1. Compare teams within the same regatta, distance, and round whenever possible.
  2. Use multiple races to reduce the impact of one-off conditions.
  3. Look at placements and progression as well as raw times.
  4. Treat times as approximate indicators, not lab-grade measurements.

For a broader view of how sport performance is studied and analyzed (and why context matters), see: International Journal of Performance Analysis in Sport (About) .

Why Isn't X Regatta Included in the Data?

I am continually adding regattas, but not all organizers publish results in formats that can be reliably extracted into structured data.

Most missing events fall into one of these categories:

  • Results are only available as images or scanned PDFs.
  • Tables are visually formatted in ways that make extraction unreliable.
  • Key information (lanes, times, team names) is missing or inconsistent.
  • The document structure changes from page to page or race to race.

When data is missing or incomplete, it is usually a source formatting issue rather than an omission.

Data Accuracy and Corrections

All results come from official published regatta documents, but errors can still happen during extraction, especially when tables split across pages or formatting changes mid-document.

If you spot a missing team, an incorrect time/place, or duplicate team names, that feedback helps improve the dataset.

The Big Picture

Dragon boat racing is messy, dynamic, and highly context-dependent. This site does not try to erase that complexity. It tries to make the history of racing easier to explore, while being honest about what the data can and cannot say.
